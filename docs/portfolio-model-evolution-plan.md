# Portfolio model evolution plan

Date: November 2, 2026
Status: Planned after the Fast Company release

## Decision

The immediate release will prioritize a focused, credible `/press1` landing page before the Fast Company article is published. The existing supporting pages will not be comprehensively rewritten as part of that release.

After the release, the site will be reorganized around Dan's connected design model, adapted from the model he created for feature[23]. The public portfolio should express the thinking in Dan's own language rather than reproduce the internal source verbatim.

## Immediate release boundary

- Keep `/press1` concise and ready for increased traffic.
- Position Dan as a senior design leader with organizational and delivery range.
- Let the model appear through business context, people and work, product direction, systems, and delivery without repeatedly naming it.
- Keep fractional leadership visible as a selective way to engage, not the primary identity.
- Defer broad rewrites of the current supporting pages and navigation.

## Later model-led work

### 1. Define the public model

Create a concise personal articulation that preserves the model's important distinctions:

- approach: how Dan understands and carries work forward
- outcomes: why the organization is investing
- problems: what buyers and teams are navigating
- contribution: the roles Dan can play inside the work
- capabilities: the expertise used
- engagement: how outside organizations can work with Dan

The model should feel present throughout the site without becoming a branded framework that every visitor must learn.

### 2. Rework the information architecture

Reconsider the current peer relationship between:

- `/operating-model`
- `/direction-and-prioritization`
- `/standards-and-scale`
- `/ai-enabled-systems`

These pages should not automatically remain four equal top-level categories. Their eventual roles may include a central approach page, supporting capability pages, specialized applications, or contextual deep dives. Page names and routes can be reconsidered after the public model is defined.

### 3. Give every page a distinct job

Each retained page should answer a different visitor question:

- What does Dan believe about design and organizational change?
- What kinds of problems can he help clarify?
- How does he connect business, people, products, and delivery?
- What evidence shows that he can do this?
- How can an organization work with him?

Pages that answer the same question should be merged, reframed, or removed.

### 4. Align navigation and cross-links

Once the page roles are settled:

- replace the current category-led navigation with a simpler visitor-led structure
- connect selected work to the relevant parts of the model
- connect model concepts to proof rather than repeating service claims
- keep engagement language contained near the point of conversion

### 5. Rewrite and redesign in one pass

Content and visual structure should be updated together. The model should influence hierarchy, sequencing, imagery, and interaction rather than appearing only as explanatory copy.

## Public-language constraints

- Do not copy the feature[23] source model word for word.
- Review ownership and attribution before publishing model-specific language or artifacts.
- Do not present contribution modes as services or sequential phases.
- Do not make fractional leadership the explanation of Dan's everyday work.
- Do not create multiple taxonomies that describe the same connected practice.
- Keep the portfolio concise enough for unfamiliar visitors arriving from press coverage.

## Completion criteria

- The landing page and supporting pages tell one coherent story.
- Each page has a distinct role in the visitor journey.
- Business context, people and work, product direction, systems, and delivery remain connected.
- The model is understandable without requiring visitors to learn internal terminology.
- Selected work provides evidence for the model.
- Fractional and advisory options remain clear but secondary.
