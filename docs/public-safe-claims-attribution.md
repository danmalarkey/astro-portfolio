# Public-Safe Claims and Attribution

This document defines how portfolio claims should be written so the site stays credible, accurate, and safe to publish while showcasing work delivered through `feature[23]` and prior leadership roles.

## Goals

- Preserve trust with executives, product leaders, and design leaders
- Avoid overclaiming ownership of team/client work
- Avoid exposing confidential or approval-sensitive details
- Make future homepage, Work, and case-study writing faster and more consistent

## Core Rules

### 1. Attribute work delivered through `feature[23]`

Use clear attribution when referencing client work delivered in the current role.

Preferred patterns:

- `through feature[23]`
- `At feature[23], I ...`
- `Selected outcomes from client engagements delivered through feature[23].`

Avoid patterns that imply solo ownership of the entire engagement unless that is strictly true and defensible.

## 2. Separate role from outcome

For public-facing summaries:

- Role line = contribution/positioning (`Design leadership through feature[23]`)
- Outcome line = what changed (`Reduced cycle time`, `Re-architected workflows`, etc.)
- Body line = mechanism/context (`how`, constraints, business reason)

This prevents outcome claims from sounding inflated or unsupported.

## 3. Prefer anonymized client labels by default

Homepage and early case-study drafts should default to anonymized labels unless explicit approval exists.

Examples:

- `Home insurance platform`
- `Enterprise logistics platform`
- `Restaurant operations platform`

Named references can be added later after approval or comfort is confirmed.

## 4. Use confidence-based metric language

Choose claim precision based on certainty.

- `Exact`: Use exact values only when verified and safe to publish
- `Rounded`: Use rounded values when exact numbers are not reliable/public-safe
- `Directional`: Use directional language when metric precision is not available

Examples:

- Exact: `Reduced cycle time from weeks to 2 days`
- Rounded: `Saved an estimated ~6,000 hours per year`
- Directional: `Compressed delivery from weeks to hours`

## 5. Do not overstate ownership

Prefer verbs that accurately reflect the role:

- `led`
- `helped lead`
- `contributed to`
- `partnered with`
- `designed`
- `reframed`
- `re-architected` (only if directly true)

Be careful with:

- `built all of`
- `single-handedly`
- `owned` (unless role/responsibility was formal and complete)

## 6. Signal currentness without overemphasizing “active”

Homepage visitors usually need to know you are credible and current, not whether each engagement is still active.

Use:

- `Chief Product Designer at feature[23]`
- present-tense capability statements
- selective availability language

Reserve `in progress` or `active` labels for deeper case-study context when needed for accuracy.

## 7. Protect confidential or sensitive details

Do not publish:

- client-sensitive screenshots/data
- approval-sensitive metrics
- internal process details that create risk

Use public-safe abstractions and artifact placeholders until approved assets are available.

## 8. Write for executive trust first

Public case and homepage claims should answer:

- What changed?
- Why did it matter?
- How did leadership/design contribute?

Avoid vague value language without a mechanism.

## Claim Templates (Reusable)

### Homepage outcomes intro

- `Selected outcomes from client engagements delivered through feature[23] and prior leadership work. Some examples are anonymized.`

### Homepage outcome card (pattern)

- Role/meta: `Design leadership through feature[23]`
- Outcome: `Helped lead a mission-critical transformation across internal operations and member-facing experiences.`
- Body: `At feature[23], I contributed to ... [context, mechanism, business reason].`

### Work/case hero attribution

- `Anonymized [industry/platform] engagement delivered through feature[23]`
- `Design leadership and execution across [scope]`
